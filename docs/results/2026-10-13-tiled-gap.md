# An efficient shared algorithm helps, but leaves world training untouched

October 13, 2026. [Declaration](../experiments/2026-10-13-tiled-gap.md)
· [Data](2026-10-13-tiled-gap.json)
· [Root-cause investigation](2026-10-13-execution-gap.md).

**A real algorithm change makes a large transposed product2.92× faster, but
saves only2.59% of complete RGB updates.** A fast matrix microbenchmark is not
a solution to the JAX gap: world training remains essentially unchanged. The
broader investigation stays open; parity is not established.

## Fixed shared algorithm

Generalize the existing aligned generator across FP32/FP16/BF16, not another
BF16-only shader. Typed full tiles use64×64 outputs, K32, vector loads, padded
shared memory, register prefetch and128 threads. Incomplete tiles retain the
checked fallback; legacy FP32 geometry stays unchanged. K partitions, rounding,
addend order and FP32 accumulation stay fixed. No geometry sweep or precision
policy change.

## Implication and next step

The type-aware algorithm path is viable on an actual training shape. It is
insufficient: RGB remains about4.16× the retained JAX baseline. This change does
not reach expensive convolution derivatives or grouped RSSM products. The prior
48.76% scalar-FP32 MAC count describes RGB, not CDP's separate mixed policy.
Neither posterior reuse nor device targets/EMA is implemented.

Next isolate convolution-backward precision/algorithm coverage, then grouped
products, with independent quantized-operand F64 derivatives and full update
checks. Reduced-precision derivatives change arithmetic; do not demand bitwise
final trajectories or silently relax strict fallback. Retain this diagnostic;
do not turn it into another geometry sweep or declare the whole goal achieved.
